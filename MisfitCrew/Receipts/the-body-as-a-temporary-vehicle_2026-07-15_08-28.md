Treats the body as a "learning device" for mind correction. Its pedagogical metaphor—spirit eternal, body illusory—replicates Gnosticism’s anti-materialism and Vedanta’s *maya*, framing the body as a transient illusion to be overcome.  

**The Magic and Science of Jewels and Stones (1915, Esoteric Mineralogy)**

**The Lost Books of the Bible (18th century compilation, Apocrypha)**

## Why The Convergence Matters  
Independent emergence across Gnostic, Theosophical, New Age, and esoteric traditions—spanning 1800 years—suggests this model isn’t cultural noise but addresses a persistent intuition: consciousness experiences itself as *in* but not *of* the body. For this pattern to hold, either human cognition reliably generates dualism as a cognitive artifact, or disparate traditions empirically discerned an ontological truth about consciousness’s relationship to matter.  

## The Hardware Glitch  
The interaction problem: if consciousness/soul is immaterial and primary, how does it causally interface with the body? No tradition resolves this. Neuroscience shows consciousness arising from physical processes, yet these systems assert consciousness *precedes* and *uses* the body—creating an explanatory gap. Either consciousness is epiphenomenal (contradicting its primacy) or the body is superfluous (contradicting its necessity as a vehicle).
